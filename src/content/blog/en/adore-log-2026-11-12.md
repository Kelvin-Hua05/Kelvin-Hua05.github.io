This is the third dev log for [ADoRE-DoE](https://github.com/Kelvin-Hua05/ADoRE-DoE). This time, I moved on to v0.3 Module 1: Active Learning. The goal is to move beyond selecting all experimental points in advance. Instead, the algorithm continuously updates its surrogate model using accumulated experimental data and recommends the next experiments accordingly.

## What I did today

- Implemented `GaussianProcess` as the surrogate model using Gaussian Process Regression (GPR). The default configuration uses a Matérn 5/2 kernel with ARD and supports hyperparameter optimization, warm starts, and reproducible random initialization.
- Implemented cross-validation predictive R² with K-fold, Repeated K-fold, and Leave-One-Out options. Each fold independently refits the model using only its training data to prevent data leakage.
- Implemented `MultiResponseStopping`, allowing multiple responses to share the same experimental points while being evaluated independently, with aggregate R² and maximum run limits used to determine when to stop.
- Implemented four acquisition strategies:
  - `i_optimal`: Uses polynomial-model I-optimality to select additional points that reduce overall prediction variance.
  - `ivr`: Integrated Predictive Variance Reduction, which measures how much a new point reduces GPR predictive variance across the design region.
  - `max_variance`: Selects the candidate with the highest GPR predictive variance as a baseline strategy.
  - `random`: Selects candidates randomly as another baseline.
- Implemented batch recommendation using Greedy Selection and Rank-1 Updates to incrementally update posterior covariance without fully refitting the GPR model after every selected point.
- Implemented `ActiveLearner` to integrate model assessment, stopping decisions, experiment recommendations, and updates with newly collected experimental results.

## Design decisions

- **Optimize global prediction improvement rather than local uncertainty alone**: The objective of Active Learning is not simply to select the point with the highest GPR uncertainty, but to improve prediction performance across the entire design region.
- **Keep I-optimality separate from GPR predictive variance**: These two criteria have different mathematical foundations. I-optimality is based on the polynomial model's design matrix, whereas GPR predictive variance is determined by its kernel and hyperparameters. They should not be treated as equivalent metrics.
- **Normalize before aggregating multiple responses**: Responses may have completely different units and numerical scales. Predictive variances are therefore normalized by each response's own data variance before being combined through a weighted average or maximum operation.
- **Use cross-validation predictive R² for stopping decisions**: Training R² is not used as the sole stopping criterion. Every response must meet its individual threshold, the aggregate R² must also satisfy its target, and the conditions must remain satisfied for three consecutive rounds.
- **Separate recommendations from experiment execution**: `recommend()` only suggests the next experimental points. It does not execute simulations or modify the dataset automatically. Users provide the actual results through `update()`.

## Strategy comparison experiment

Compared Random, Maximum Predictive Variance, IVR, and I-optimal Augmentation using three factors and three synthetic functions.

Experimental setup:
- Initial design: 12 Latin Hypercube points.
- Additional experiments: 12 sequential runs, one point per iteration.
- Final dataset: 24 runs.
- Evaluation set: A fixed set of 1,000 points.
- Repetitions: 8 random seeds.
- Evaluation metric: NRMSE (RMSE normalized by the standard deviation of the true values).

Main results:

| Function type | Random | Max Variance | IVR | I-optimal |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| Smooth | 0.0319 | 0.0111 | 0.0117 | 0.0110 |
| Nonlinear | 0.3189 | 0.3385 | 0.3039 | 0.3303 |
| Localized | 0.5992 | 0.8462 | 0.6737 | 0.8814 |

Values represent the mean NRMSE across 8 seeds. Lower values indicate better predictive performance.

Observations:
- Smooth: All three model-based strategies substantially outperform Random, with only minor differences between them.
- Nonlinear: IVR achieves a slightly better average result than Random, but the difference remains within the variation across seeds. No clear winner can be established.
- Localized: Random performs best. IVR outperforms Max Variance but does not consistently outperform Random, while I-optimal produces the weakest result.

These results indicate that acquisition strategy performance depends on the shape of the underlying function. No single strategy should be assumed to perform best for every problem.

## Open issues

- **No universally superior acquisition strategy has been identified**: Current experiments do not establish that IVR can consistently outperform both Random and Max Variance. More function types and experimental settings are needed.
- **Point selection for localized peaks**: Max Variance and I-optimal may favor boundary or corner points rather than effectively capturing localized peaks inside the design region.
- **GPR hyperparameter constraints**: Hyperparameter optimization is currently bounded. Extreme functions may push parameters to their limits. If optimization fails, the implementation falls back to default parameters and issues a warning.
- **Candidate search limitations**: Recommendations are selected from a finite candidate set, which has a limited default size. Therefore, the selected point is not guaranteed to be the global optimum over the continuous design space.
- **Predictive R² stability**: With very few experimental observations, cross-validation predictive R² may still have substantial estimation variance. Requiring three consecutive successful rounds reduces false stopping decisions but does not eliminate them.
- A complete numerical comparison against scikit-learn or other GPR implementations has not yet been performed.

## Next steps

- Expand strategy comparison experiments with additional function shapes, factor counts, and initial design configurations.
- Investigate acquisition strategies that are more suitable for localized peaks and complex nonlinear functions.
- Further evaluate the efficiency and predictive improvements of batch selection under different batch sizes.
- Connect the accumulated Active Learning dataset to the final RSM modeling and diagnostic modules to investigate whether sequential selection improves the predictive performance of the final RSM model.
- Proceed to the Benchmark phase to quantify the differences between Adaptive DOE and Traditional DOE under fixed experimental budgets and target accuracy requirements.
